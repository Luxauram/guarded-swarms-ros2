# guarded_swarms_gz

The Gazebo side of the coastal scenario: the world, and the two models the
repository provides. The UAVs use `x500_lidar_front`, a model of PX4 with a
forward lidar, which the repository does not ship.

| Item                 | Purpose                                                                   |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `worlds/coastal.sdf` | The coastal world, generated by `scripts/world/gen_coastal.py`            |
| `models/r1_rover`    | The R1 rover of PX4 with a frontal lidar, for the Safety Board of the UGV |
| `models/intruder`    | The intruder, moved by `intruder_node`                                    |

The package installs `worlds/` and `models/` to `share/guarded_swarms_gz`.
`scripts/sim/gz_launch.sh` puts the two folders of the source tree on
`GZ_SIM_RESOURCE_PATH` before those of PX4, so that Gazebo finds the world and
takes these models in place of PX4's, without copying anything into PX4.

## coastal.sdf

A coastal perimeter, monitored by the radar, the RF mast, the acoustic buoys
and the EO camera of UAV0, with a harbour, a town and a shore road. The area is
400 × 400 m, compressed from the kilometres a real coastal radar covers so that
the PX4 simulation of every vehicle runs on one workstation. The compression is
geometric only: the ranges, thresholds and time windows are parameters of the
agents and of the transitions, not of the world.

The world is in ENU: Gazebo x points east and y north. PX4 works in NED, with
north along Gazebo y and east along Gazebo x, so a rover that drives along the
shore varies the second, east component of its waypoints.

| y, m         | What lies there                                                                         |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| +200 to 0    | The sea; the intruder starts at (0, 180, 15)                                            |
| +64 to +76   | The inner ring of five acoustic buoys                                                   |
| +120 to +132 | An outer ring of four acoustic buoys                                                    |
| +16 to +34   | The harbour: the breakwater, the piers and the moored boats; the lighthouse at (52, 12) |
| 0            | The shoreline, the beach and the quay                                                   |
| -8           | The promenade                                                                           |
| -14          | The shore road, which the UGV patrols, with three obstacles                             |
| -24          | The two UAV launch pads, at x = -24 and 24, and four warehouses                         |
| -46 to -118  | The town: four rows of buildings, 8 to 34 m high                                        |
| -160         | The radar on its hill at x = 0, the RF mast at x = -150                                 |

The scenery is grouped into nested models, so that the entity tree of Gazebo
shows a few folders instead of a flat list of a hundred boxes:

| Group        | Content                                                                                                          |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `terrain`    | The surfaces of the sea, the inland, the beach, the promenade, the shore road and the streets, without collision |
| `town`       | 38 buildings, 8 to 34 m high, in four rows of ten places with two open squares                                   |
| `harbour`    | The breakwater and its arm, two piers, the quay, four warehouses and six moored boats                            |
| `sensing`    | The radar, the RF mast with its antennas, the nine acoustic buoys and the lighthouse                             |
| `flight_ops` | The two UAV launch pads, without collision                                                                       |
| `obstacles`  | A beached container at (62, -14), shore rocks at (-78, -14) and a road barrier at (-8, -14), on the shore road   |

The sensing infrastructure is only a visual reference: what each sensor
detects is decided by its agent, from the agent's own parameters. The radar
and the RF mast stand where their agents place them, and the five buoys of the
inner ring are those of the acoustic agent; the outer ring is scenery only.

The intruder is not in a group, because Gazebo does not apply velocity
commands to nested models: it stands at world level, at (0, 180, 15), the
default ingress point. The vehicles of PX4 are spawned at run time, at world
level too.

The physics runs ODE with a step of 2 ms, at 500 steps per second for a
real-time factor of 1. The spherical coordinates are those of the default
world of PX4, so the state PX4 derives from GPS matches its other worlds.

### Generating the world

`coastal.sdf` is generated: change `scripts/world/gen_coastal.py`, not the file.

```bash
make gen-coastal
```

The generator is deterministic: a fixed seed, 20260723, drives every random
draw, the sizes and heights of the buildings among them, so every regeneration
gives a byte-identical file, which matters because the experiments are recorded
against it. The `COASTAL_OUT` environment variable writes the file elsewhere.
Before writing, the generator checks that the spawn points of UAV0, UAV1 and
the UGV are at least 6 m from every solid, prints the clearance of each, and
stops if one is closer.

## r1_rover

The R1 rover of PX4, with a frontal lidar added for the Safety Board of the UGV:

| Property | Value                                        |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Link     | `lidar_sensor_link`, 0.5 m ahead of the base |
| Sensor   | `lidar`, a `gpu_lidar`, at 10 Hz             |
| Field    | 180°, 640 horizontal samples, one vertical   |
| Range    | 0.08 to 100 m, Gaussian noise of 0.01 m      |

PX4 runs it as `gz_r1_rover`. Its scan is published on
`/world/coastal/model/r1_rover_<i>/link/lidar_sensor_link/sensor/lidar/scan`,
which `coastal_pipeline.launch.py` brings into ROS 2 (see
[`guarded_swarms_bringup`](guarded_swarms_bringup.md#coastal_pipelinelaunchpy)).
`make setup-ugv` checks that the model carries its lidar.

## intruder

A small quadrotor shape, with a box of collision of 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.2 m, moved
kinematically rather than by PX4: the trajectory repeats exactly on every run,
and no further PX4 instance slows the simulation. Its size and radar
cross-section are not modelled.

| Plugin              | Role                                                                                                           |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `VelocityControl`   | Applies the velocity that `intruder_node` publishes on `/model/intruder/cmd_vel`                               |
| `OdometryPublisher` | Publishes the odometry of the model on `/model/intruder/odometry`, and its transform on `/model/intruder/pose` |

Gravity is off on its link, so the target holds its altitude without a thrust
model.

No node reads the odometry. The ground truth that the sensing agents read is
the trajectory `intruder_node` integrates from its commanded velocity (see
[`guarded_swarms_control`](guarded_swarms_control.md#intruder_node)). With the
default ingress point, where the world places the model, the model follows that
trajectory. With another ingress point the model starts at (0, 180, 15) all
the same, and only the trajectory of `intruder_node` follows the ingress.
